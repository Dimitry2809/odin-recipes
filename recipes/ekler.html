Choux pastries

History of origin

It all began in France, at the court of Catherine de' Medici. The court pastry chef prepared the dough in a saucepan over the fire. He formed it into flat cakes and placed them in the oven. As they puffed up, a hollow space formed inside; They put pear jam in there and got a sweet dessert.

It took the French centuries to come up with the idea of ​​cooking a mixture of eggs and flour in milk to create "choux pastry" (or "cooked dough"); around the same time, they decided to shape this dough into elongated sticks, which they dubbed "lightning bolts" (*éclairs*).

Dough

  • Flour - 150 gr
  • Butter - 100 gr
  • Water - 250 ml
  • Eggs - 4
  • Salt - 1/2 teaspoon

Pour the water into a pot and add the butter and salt. Bring to a boil. Reduce the heat to the lowest setting and add all the flour at once. Stir vigorously until the dough stops sticking to the sides of the pot. Transfer the dough to another container and let it cool to room temperature. Then, incorporate the eggs into the dough, one by one, mixing thoroughly after each addition until the surface of the dough appears matte. Using a piping bag, pipe the dough onto a baking sheet in the shape of "sausages" (elongated strips). Bake in an oven preheated to 180–200°C (356–392°F) for 35–45 minutes. Afterward, turn off the oven and leave them inside for another 40 minutes. Finally, remove them from the oven. If you take them out too soon, the sudden change in temperature may cause them to collapse.
